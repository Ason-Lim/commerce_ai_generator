### Official representations

- Federal Register:
  <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03829/continuing-the-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries>
- GovInfo official PDF:

The White House page and Federal Register publication are official

### Directly established scope and time facts

- The modification made by Executive Order 14388 applies to goods entered for
  consumption, or withdrawn from warehouse for consumption, on or after
  `2026-02-24 12:01 a.m. EST`.
- The order continues suspension of the duty-free de minimis exemption under
  `19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(C)`.
- For articles not covered by `50 U.S.C. 1702(b)`, the order states that the
  exemption does not apply regardless of value, country of origin, mode of
  transportation, or method of entry.
- Non-postal covered shipments are subject to applicable duties, taxes, fees,
  exactions, and charges.
- Covered shipments that previously qualified for the exemption require an
  appropriate ACE entry type, except for the transitional international-postal
  treatment specified by the order.
- The order separately addresses international postal shipments and directs
  duty collection under its amended postal provisions.

### Directly established scope and time facts

- The proclamation imposed a temporary `10 percent ad valorem` import
  surcharge, subject to stated exceptions.
- The surcharge applied from `2026-02-24 12:01 a.m. EST`.
- The proclamation stated that the surcharge would continue through
  `2026-07-24 12:01 a.m. EDT`, unless earlier suspended, modified, or
  terminated, or extended by an Act of Congress.

  applicable duties, taxes, fees, exactions, and charges except where the
  proclamation provided otherwise.

after `2026-07-24`.

### Directly established scope facts

- The rule indefinitely suspends the de minimis administrative exemption for

- Modification or revocation of the regulatory suspension is to be announced
  in the Federal Register.

- GovInfo official PDF:
  <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-24/pdf/2026-12669.pdf>

### Directly established scope facts

- The rule indefinitely suspends the de minimis administrative exemption for
  merchandise valued at `$800 or less` arriving through the international
  postal network.
- The suspension concerns merchandise imported by one person on one day under
  `19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(C)`.
- The rule establishes a new postal informal entry process for qualifying
  merchandise valued at `$2,500 or less`.
- The filing party must have the right to make entry or be an appropriately
  designated licensed customs broker.
- The informal-mail process requires a qualifying entry bond and specified
  classification, value, duty, carrier, tracking, and arrival data.
- Shipments subject to disqualifying requirements, including specified
  AD/CVD or quota conditions, require another appropriate entry procedure.
- Modification or revocation of the regulatory suspension is to be announced
  in the Federal Register.
- The rule does not change the separate exemptions for qualifying bona fide
  gifts under `19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(A)` or qualifying traveler articles under
  `19 U.S.C. 1321(a)(2)(B)`.

Evidence status: `VERIFIED`

## Source facts

The following are source facts supported directly by the official instruments:

1. The former `$800 or less` commercial de minimis assumption is not available
   as a general duty-free rule for covered non-postal or international-postal
   shipments.
2. Non-postal and international-postal shipments are governed by distinct CBP
   entry procedures.
3. International-postal Entry Type 13 was announced as a test beginning
   `2026-09-22`, not as an already-operational process on `2026-08-30`.
4. The temporary `10 percent` surcharge was time-limited through
   `2026-07-24` and is not a current fixed general postal rate.
5. The statutory basis for the relevant de minimis exemption is scheduled for
   termination on `2027-07-01`.

## Project interpretation

The Commerce AI Generator must not model United States-bound covered commercial
shipments using an unconditional rule that goods valued at `$800 or less` are
duty free.

Any future landed-cost or route recommendation should distinguish at least:

- international postal versus non-postal transportation;
- the applicable entry procedure;
- HTSUS classification;
- country of origin;
- shipment value;
- applicable ordinary and additional duties;
- PGA, AD/CVD, quota, and other eligibility constraints; and
- the policy-effective date relevant to the transaction.

This interpretation is a research conclusion only. It is not canonical
admission and does not authorize implementation or runtime behavior.
